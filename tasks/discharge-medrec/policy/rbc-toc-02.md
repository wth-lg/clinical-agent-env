RBC-TOC-02 Medication reconciliation after hospital discharge

Applies when patients return from a hospital stay and each one's outpatient medication list must match their discharge documentation before the post-discharge visit. On a worklist of discharged patients, reconcile each patient on their own.

§1 Sources
Reconcile against the discharge documentation of the hospital stay that just ended: the discharge summary and any addendum to it. Where a signed addendum and the summary differ, the latest signed addendum rules.

§2 Conditional instructions
Act on a conditional instruction when results already in the chart satisfy its condition: a result in the patient's results, or a laboratory report filed to the chart. Otherwise create a Task due when the result is expected.

§3 Formulary substitutions
An inpatient formulary substitution marked as such reverts to the home drug: keep the home drug and do not order the substitute.

§4 Drug names
A brand name, a generic name and a release-form abbreviation (for example "SR" or "CR") for the same ingredient name the same drug. A discharge line for a drug the patient already has an order for applies to that order: at the same dose it means no change, and at another dose it changes that order (§6). It never adds a second order of the same drug.

§5 Home medications
The patient's home medications are the clinic's active and held orders, and every medication the patient's pharmacy fill histories show filled in the 180 days before admission.
(a) A home medication the discharge documentation does not address must not be changed or ordered: message the PCP about it, with its order in the message's about, or, for a medication with no clinic order, the fill history that shows it.
(b) A home medication a fill history lists, whose fills cover less than 80% of the 180 days before admission, or of the days from its first fill to admission when that fill falls inside them, is not being taken as prescribed (each fill covers its days' supply from its fill date). Make no change to it, even when the discharge documentation continues it: message the PCP about it, with its order in the message's about.

§6 Making changes
Change a dose by modifying the existing order (stop it and replace it); resume a held order rather than duplicating it. Document each patient's reconciliation in a progress note for that patient: each change and the document it comes from, citing the documents and results relied on.

§7 Potassium
Do not start or resume a potassium-raising drug (a mineralocorticoid antagonist, an ACE inhibitor or ARB, or a potassium supplement) when the most recent potassium after discharge is above 5.0 mmol/L, whatever the discharge documents say. Keep a held order held, place no new order for such a drug, and create a Task for the PCP due 2 to 7 days after that potassium result, when the next potassium is expected.

§8 Arrivals during reconciliation
Results, corrections and messages can arrive in your queues while you work. Before you finish, review anything that arrived after you started. If it changes a decision you already recorded under this protocol, correct that decision (change, hold or stop the order, or send a corrected message) and record why. If it does not change the decision under this protocol, leave the decision in place.
